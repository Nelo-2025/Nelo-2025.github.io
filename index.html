About the Stay and Play Programme

The Stay and Play programme is a weekly community initiative designed to support families with young children. It provides a safe and engaging environment for children to play and interact with peers, while offering parents opportunities to connect and build relationships within the community.

Core Programme Aims

01

Provide a safe, nurturing, and engaging space for children to explore and develop

02

Support child development across cognitive, physical, emotional, and social domains

03

Promote the emotional and mental well-being of parents and caregivers

04

Reduce social isolation and foster peer connection among families

05

Ensure consistent, evidence-informed session delivery aligned with early years best practice

As a volunteer, I have been actively involved in supporting the delivery of these sessions, ensuring that they meet the needs of the community and align with the programme's core aims. Recognising a gap in how programme impact was being captured, I designed and implemented an analytical framework to evaluate delivery against these objectives.

My Leadership Contributions

Identifying the Need

Through regular attendance at sessions, I observed that while practitioners delivered high-quality facilitation, there was no systematic method for reviewing whether session content consistently reflected the programme's thematic focus areas. Valuable session summaries were being written but not analysed. I took the initiative to design a solution.

Designing the Analytical Framework

I independently designed a Python-based NLP pipeline to analyse session summaries. The framework was built with three core objectives:

Objectivity

Remove manual bias from thematic assessment

Reproducibility

Enable any future volunteer to run the same analysis

Accessibility

Produce outputs that non-technical stakeholders can interpret

Pipeline Components

Text Cleaning & Tokenisation

Standardise session notes; remove noise and personally identifiable information

Named Entity Recognition (NER)

Anonymise participant names automatically using spaCy to uphold data ethics

Word Frequency Analysis

Identify most-used language across sessions to surface recurring priorities

Sentiment Analysis (TextBlob)

Measure emotional tone of each session to track well-being trajectory over time

LDA Topic Modelling

Automatically discover latent themes without manual labelling, providing objective evidence of thematic alignment

Visualisation

Generate charts and word clouds for reporting to coordinators and funders

Leading Through Collaboration

I presented my proposed framework to the programme coordinator before implementation, seeking feedback and alignment with safeguarding obligations. I explained the anonymisation approach (using NER to strip participant names) and obtained informal sign-off to proceed with analysis of existing session notes. This demonstrated an ability to lead with accountability — bringing others along rather than acting unilaterally.

Delivering Measurable Output

The completed analysis produced three tangible outputs that the organisation can continue to use:

01

Reproducible Analysis

A Jupyter notebook hosted on GitHub, enabling ongoing analysis of future session summaries

02

Impact Report

A thematic alignment report mapping session language to the programme's five core focus areas

03

Visualisation Suite

Topic distribution charts, sentiment trend graphs, and word clouds ready for funding applications

These outputs provide the programme with a data-driven method to demonstrate impact and inform continuous improvement efforts.

Thematic Findings

The LDA topic model, applied across all available session summaries, identified five dominant themes. These themes were assessed against the programme's stated objectives to evaluate thematic alignment:

Safe Environment

Key Indicators: Safeguarding language, structured boundaries, consistent routines

Evidence: Session notes consistently referenced boundary-setting, structured transitions, and child-protective practices

Nurturing Atmosphere

Key Indicators: Emotional warmth, responsive caregiving, positive reinforcement

Evidence: Recurring vocabulary around encouragement and patience throughout session summaries

Child Development

Key Indicators: Motor skills, cognitive engagement, age-appropriate activities

Evidence: Activity descriptions mapped to developmental milestones; sensory and creative play featured in majority of sessions

Parental Wellbeing

Key Indicators: Peer support, reduced isolation, caregiver confidence

Evidence: Parent-focused themes emerged strongly; peer connection and shared experience language appeared across sessions

Community Engagement

Key Indicators: Group cohesion, social bonding, inclusive participation

Evidence: Cross-session analysis revealed consistent community-building language, shared rituals, and group identity markers

The convergence of algorithmically-discovered topics with the programme's stated mission provides objective evidence that Stay and Play is delivering on its core purpose. This finding is significant for funding bids, which increasingly require data-backed impact evidence.

Reflection

This experience reinforced my belief that data and technology, when applied thoughtfully, can amplify the impact of community organisations that often lack analytical capacity. Volunteering with Stay and Play challenged me to translate technical skills into genuine social value — a translation that requires empathy, communication, and an understanding of organisational context, not just technical proficiency.

The project also deepened my appreciation for the complexity of early years community work. Watching the language of safety, nurture, and belonging emerge organically from the data — rather than being imposed by a manual coding framework — provided powerful validation of the practitioners' work.

Going forward, I intend to develop the framework further by building an automated monthly reporting tool and an interactive dashboard so that coordinators can monitor thematic alignment in real time without technical support.

Conclusion

My voluntary contribution to Stay and Play demonstrates leadership in action: identifying a gap, designing a solution, collaborating with stakeholders, and delivering outputs of lasting value to the organisation. The analytical framework I built provides the programme with an evidence base it previously lacked — one that can support funding bids, practitioner reflection, and ongoing quality assurance.

This project is available in full on GitHub as an open-source resource, reflecting a commitment to knowledge-sharing and reproducibility that is central to responsible data practice.
